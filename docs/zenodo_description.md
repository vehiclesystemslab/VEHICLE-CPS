# Zenodo Description — VEHICLE-CPS v1.1

VEHICLE-CPS — Civil Protection Systems presents a projection-governed civil protection architecture for studying high-density human environments as relational fields under tension. The work extends the Borda Milan Pyramid and the VEHICLE Formula-as-Architecture into the domain of environmental safety, perimeter coherence, public event resilience and controlled human response.

The central hypothesis of VEHICLE-CPS is that a mass human environment is not only a crowd, but a dynamic relational system composed of people, access points, protected zones, routes, operational teams, credentials, devices and critical areas. When such a system begins to lose coherence, early signals may appear as route pressure, anomalous concentration, perimeter deformation, disconnected operational nodes, evacuation stress or incompatible movement patterns.

This version reframes VEHICLE-CPS as a scientific and operational research framework rather than a surveillance technology. The proposed system does not identify attackers, profile individuals, perform facial recognition or automate coercive action. Instead, it is designed as a decision-support layer under mandatory human supervision, helping authorized teams interpret environmental tension, classify operational regimes and coordinate safer responses.

The document introduces the application of VEHICLE’s structured node state E.I.A.R.(V), dual tension modeling, projection-governed correction and the operational taxonomy P0–P6 for civil protection states. These regimes range from structural rest and controlled normal flow to localized review, anomalous concentration, critical incoherence, reinforcement and controlled evacuation.

VEHICLE-CPS is intended as a foundation for simulation, validation and future partnership development. The proposed next stage is a reproducible synthetic simulation program involving 1,000–10,000 human nodes, designed to test computational feasibility, tension evolution, anomaly-detection timing, return-to-safe-state dynamics, ethical constraints and operational interpretability before any physical pilot.

This work may be of interest to researchers and institutions working in complex systems, civil protection, emergency management, crowd dynamics, public event safety, graph-based modeling, human-in-the-loop decision support, resilient infrastructure, applied mathematics, security engineering and ethical AI governance.
